Build plan · written against the code, not the intent

Studio, Lab, Exchange

Where SoulSonus actually stands today, the three layers it is becoming, and the order the remaining work has to happen in so that nothing gets plugged in sideways later.

Every status below was read from main, not recalled Verification detail lives in the Signal Map; this is the plan

What is being built

Not a DAW with AI features bolted on. Three connected layers, where the output of each becomes an input to the next — and where the person, not the model, is the source.

LAYER ONE

Create music

Every legitimate path in. Perform with mouth or body, play MIDI, record audio, import stems, draw notes, use samples, synths, session players, a generative engine. The mouth/body workflow is the signature entry point, never a requirement.

LAYER TWO

Create sounds

The Sound Lab. Capture a source, shape it, make variations, build it into a kit or a multi-sampled instrument or a patch. Sometimes a model is trained; usually it is sound design. The plan says which is which.

LAYER THREE

Publish sounds

A governed asset exchange, not a sample store. A creator's kit, a musician's performance intelligence, a vocalist's licensed identity — each carrying its own rights, lineage and permitted uses.

Four rules that decide how the code is written

Each of these is cheap to honour now and expensive to retrofit. They are here because getting any one of them wrong produces a working product with the wrong shape.

R1

A generative engine is never the product abstraction. ACE is wired behind a SessionPlayer, not to the UI. The creator addresses a bass player; the bass player decides the task, the instruction and the freedom, and calls whatever renderer is behind it. Wire ACE to the interface and you get "Generate Bass" — a different product, and one that cannot swap renderers later.

R2

A marketplace asset is the same object as a studio asset. A kick bought from another creator is the same SoundAsset the DAW already understands — not a store item that has to be converted. Private asset to published asset is one flag, not a second technology stack.

R3

Do not call it training unless a model is being trained. Designing a sound, building a multi-sample instrument, generating variations and adapting a model are four different operations. One surface can present them together; the code must not pretend they are the same thing, and the creator must not be told a model learned something when a file was written.

R4

Signing belongs to SeedSignature. It is a separate product that signs across the ecosystem, so SoulSonus stubs it behind a seam and states plainly that it is not integrated. What SoulSonus owns is the creator's style profile — calibration, provenance, decisions. Those are two layers, and today's code tangles them.

Running alongside, not underneath: Sound Lab takes a captured or designed source through shaping, variation and packaging into a SoundAsset, attaches its rights, and publishes it — private, to a project, or to the exchange. The standard a player has to meet

Not a feature list — an acceptance bar, in the creator's words: the talent was so enormous they didn't have to be there when the song was created, but once they heard it, it was like they created it with you. Everything below follows from that sentence, and it is a harder bar than “produces a valid bass line”.

1

He hears the song, not a description of it. This is the mechanism, not a nicety. A brief carrying tempo and key is a spec sheet; a brief carrying the actual parts is a playback. The context is the single thing most responsible for whether a take belongs — which makes it where the engineering effort goes.

2

He gets it on the first pass. Cold, no rehearsal, one hearing. A player who needs six rerolls to land something usable has not met the bar — that is the slot machine, and it is the interaction model this product exists to refuse. Spend on what he hears, not on how many times you can ask again.

3

He finds his place rather than taking the song over. The professional who wasn't there does not rewrite what you made. He plays in the pocket that already exists, in the register that leaves room, answering what the vocal is doing. The take is additive by construction and the grant is how much room he is given — discipline is the trait being modelled, and overplaying is a failure even when the notes are good.

4

It is a whole department, not a rhythm section. Background singers, horns, strings, percussion — anything a session department could send. Two of those hand back audio rather than notes, which is why that split sits in the type system from the first commit.

The session department

The roster has two kinds of player and one interface. An agent answers in seconds; a person answers in days. Structurally that is the only difference — the brief, the grant, the check and the candidate flow were never about who is holding the instrument.

CALLABLE NOW

Agent players

Instant, always available, learning the creator's taste over time. Sound comes from a bank or a renderer; judgement comes from a reasoning model given the real session.

CALLABLE SOON

Live musicians

A real player takes the same brief, records, and their take arrives as a candidate to audition and accept. A SessionPlayer whose generateTake() resolves in three days instead of nine seconds.

THE LOOP

Why both

Every human take a creator picks over another is a labelled preference pair on a real session context, produced by a professional. Human players are how the agent band learns — credited and paid, permanently, rather than scraped.

What gets recorded when a person plays

Not a payment system and not a rights model — a record honest enough that whatever arrangement the two parties reach is provable later. Work for hire is the norm and stays the easy default; the record exists so the exception is possible.

·

Who played, under which brief, under which grant. The grant is the part that matters most and it is already stored: PLAY EXACTLY is pure performance and work for hire is clean; PLAY WHAT YOU FEEL means the player may have composed something, and composition is where a publishing claim lives. The system knows which was given, so it can raise the terms conversation at the moment both parties are still happy rather than two years later.

·

The hash of the take that was actually delivered. Terms attach to a specific performance, and the bytes behind the hash are the bytes that were played.

·

What was agreed, in the parties' own words. Free text before it is ever a dropdown. A field two people agreed and signed beats a schema that forces a wrong answer, and the structure can be derived later from real agreements rather than guessed now.

·

Training consent, asked separately. ResourceAdmissionRecord.trainingPermission already exists beside commercialAllowed, and the separation is the point: use my take on your record, do not train on it is a coherent answer, and so is train on it, here is the rate. Musicians are currently trained on without being asked. Asking is a reason for good players to be here.

The order, and why it is this order

Sequenced by dependency and by what each one unblocks — not by size. The first four need no GPU and no external service.

  1. The seed track receives its own notes

    Where your creativity starts, and it is a routing bug rather than a feature: the seed track's instrument is never in the class list the router checks. Small, contained, and it stops the studio contradicting itself the moment you press record.

    Blocks nothing · fixes the first thing you touchNo host

  2. The SessionPlayer layer

    Role, musical context, freedom level, creator locks, generateTake(). Decided before ACE is finalised, because it determines how a Lego request is framed, what metadata travels, which track owns the output, and whether a result arrives as an "AI generation" or as a session player's take. The context has to carry the other parts — hearing the song is the job.

    R1 · blocks all ACE workNo host

  3. Sampled instruments

    The largest believability gain available, and every existing track benefits whether or not the band is ever called. It is also what makes a session player's take worth listening to — a bass line is only as good as the bass. Needs a sound bank and a licence decision, not a GPU.

    Unblocks: a player that sounds realNeeds a sound bank

  4. The style profile, from real data

    Calibration fingerprint, note provenance, and every accept and reject — kept and connected, replacing the identical literal array every creator currently gets. This is the substrate the band eventually learns you from, and the data is already being generated and discarded. Separate from signing.

    Unblocks: a band that plays like youNo host

  5. The private lane — call a musician you know

    Brief a player you already have a number for, they record, the take arrives as a candidate. No discovery, no booking, no escrow, no reputation — and therefore no obligations to anyone you have not met. Small, usable immediately, and it sits on the same seam the marketplace will later grow behind. The delivery record is built here: who, which grant, the hash, the agreed terms in their own words, training consent as its own answer.

    Ships the capability without the marketplaceAfter: 02

  6. The SeedSignature seam

    One provider interface, the local hashing demoted to an openly-labelled placeholder, and a third status so the footer stops claiming VERIFIED on SoulSonus's own arithmetic. Small, and it stops anything else being built on top of a stand-in.

    R4Waits on: the real contract

  7. Full-composition beatbox extraction

    Per-band onset detection so a kick and a hat 20 ms apart become two events instead of one, plus Basic Pitch over the same take for the sustained and pitched layer. Turns one performance into its proper tracks. Best tuned against a real take of yours rather than a synthetic one.

    No hostWants: a real beatbox take

  8. Sound Lab

    Capture, shape, variate, build, and publish — with R3 honoured throughout, so a creator is told which of those four operations actually happened. Depends on the asset model being settled first, which it now is.

    Layer twoNo host

  9. ACE, in its own order

    extract first — its output is the thing the timeline can already hold. Then cover and repaint. Then lego, arriving behind a session player rather than as a button. Then complete. All of it waits on a host, and none of it changes the shape once step 02 exists.

    Needs a GPU hostAfter: 02

  10. The exchange

    Publish path, discovery, and the terms the record does not carry yet — royalty, attribution, derivative use. Built on the same SoundAsset the studio plays, per R2, so a published asset is a permission change and not a second stack.

    Layer threeAfter: 07

Decisions I need from you

Each of these changes what gets built rather than how well. I would rather ask than guess and refactor.

The take vocabulary

Pocket, melodic and adventurous are dispositions. You said there are more — fills, solos, and others. A real call is often for a job: fill into the chorus, take a solo over the turnaround, walk it, lay out for eight, double the vocal, hold a pad under it. Some of those are bar-ranged by nature. I would rather take that vocabulary from you than invent it, because you have made those calls in a room.

The sound bank

Sampled instruments need a bank, and that is a licensing decision as much as a technical one. A permissively licensed general bank to start? Something you own or commission? This affects step 03 and everything the band sounds like.

A real beatbox take

For step 06 I can synthesise a test signal with known contents, but a synthetic beatbox is not a beatbox. A take of you performing a full composition would let the extractor be tuned against what you actually do.

SeedSignature's contract

When it is ready: endpoints, auth model, what a signed record looks like coming back, and how a lineage chain crosses products. Until then the seam holds the shape and claims nothing.
